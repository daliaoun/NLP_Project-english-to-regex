Limitations and next steps

What this study cannot say, and what comes next

Limitations

  • Functional accuracy is a sampling test on about 600 strings, not a proof of equivalence
  • Two seeds for the main comparison and one for the variants, so small gaps are noise
  • Our scores are not directly comparable with the paper’s DFA-based accuracy of 58.2%
  • Negation and grouping stay hard

Next steps

  • Exact DFA equivalence as the metric
  • Syntax-aware decoding: balanced parentheses and operator scope, or a tree decoder
  • A Transformer encoder-decoder for comparison
  • Seed ensembles and more paraphrase data

15 · Limitations

Being honest about limits matters here. Our functional accuracy is a sampling test and some references accept nothing, so it can overstate. The paper we compare with uses an exact equivalence check and a different split, so the two numbers are only in the same ballpark. We ran two seeds for the main comparison, which is enough to see that attention helps but not to rank the smaller variations. The most promising next step is to constrain the decoder with the regex grammar so that grouping and negation scope are handled structurally.
